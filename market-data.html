Space Economy Size, Launch Costs & Satellite Markets — Market Data

The space economy crossed $686 billion in 2025, and the striking part is not the total but the split: commercial revenue now runs at nearly four times government spending. Space stopped being a government program with contractors some time ago. It is a commercial market with government customers, and the pricing in this page reflects that order.

Launch prices anchor the cost side. A Falcon 9 lists at $74 million; a single Artemis launch, by the NASA Inspector General's count, costs $4.1 billion. The gap between those two numbers is not a rounding difference — it is the difference between a reusable commercial vehicle and a government exploration architecture, and it explains most of what follows in this market.

Methodology

Included: Space Foundation annual totals and segment growth (as reported in public summaries of the 2026 report), published SpaceX list prices, the NASA Inspector General's Artemis launch cost, and NASA budget figures from The Planetary Society's public guide. Excluded: Starship commercial pricing — SpaceX has published none — and any satellite market forecast from a paywalled research house. Alternate market-size estimates are shown side by side with their definitions, because the totals differ by scope, not by arithmetic.

Trend labels: “Baseline (October 2026) — trend tracking starts with the next refresh” means no prior-period figure from the same source was available this session, so no movement is claimed. Where a prior figure is shown, the trend compares the current figure with that prior figure from the same source.
